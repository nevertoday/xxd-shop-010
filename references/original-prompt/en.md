# XXD Shop 010 · Original brief (English reading translation)

> Reading translation only. The canonical runtime prompt is [zh-CN.md](zh-CN.md).

Generate a white-background product label image for e-commerce use, presenting a clean, clear, refined and easy-to-read e-commerce selling-point label style.

First analyse the product information, product image, flavour attributes, category features, core selling points and consumption scene supplied by the user, then decide the label content, visual focus and copy hierarchy.

Use a pure white or near-white background. The subject is a standalone label card or a decorative outlined label. The label shape may be simple, orderly and refined, or carry a slight stylistic feel according to the product's tone, but its outline must be clear, its structure complete and easy to carry information.

The product image must be the visual centre and merge with the label: the product is shown embedded inside the label, while key parts are allowed to break naturally past the label's edge, creating a "leaping out of the label" effect. The part that crosses the edge should be reasonable, obvious and natural, enhancing depth, appetite or attraction without breaking the overall neatness. The product must be clear, real and attractive, preferably showing its most appealing angle, cross-section, texture or flavour feature.

The layout follows clear e-commerce label logic, with concentrated rather than scattered information. Build a clear hierarchy:
at the top, a short lead-in, a flavour-impression word or an emotional short line;
in the middle, the product name, core flavour name or main title, which must be prominent and readable;
at the bottom, supporting English, small selling points, flavour notes, ingredient highlights or brief additions.
Small round badges, corner marks or emblems may supplement one key selling point, but in restrained number.

If the user has not provided a clear copy plan, intelligently generate concise, persuasive, not overly exaggerated label copy from the product category, flavour, consumption scene and e-commerce conventions. The copy first solves three things: what this is, what is special about it, and why it is worth choosing. The language is short, precise and easy to scan, avoiding lengthy explanations.

If the user has provided a clear copy plan, first analyse its structure and priorities, distil the main title, subtitle, selling-point lines, supplementary information and emphasis words, then land them in a clearer form better suited to a label. Compressing, organising, strengthening hierarchy and improving readability are allowed without changing the core meaning.

Type and text–image relationships need clear logic. The main title is strongest, complete in letterform, prominent and memorable; supporting information clearly recedes; Chinese and English may form a hierarchy contrast but must not compete. Text must not cover the product's most tempting part or chop the image into too many pieces.

Colour starts from the product itself: label base, stroke, decorative lines and text colours unfold naturally around the product's flavour or brand character. Keep clear contrast and refinement on the white background, avoiding busy backgrounds and too many colours. The label colour should help the product stand out, not steal the show.

Compositionally, the label as a whole should be standalone and complete, suitable for use alone as an e-commerce information module. The visual order is clear: product first, then the main title, then the selling-point additions. The overall effect should be like a mature e-commerce product label with product recognisability, persuasive selling points and good visual appeal.

Avoid: complex backgrounds, overly fancy labels, too much copy, confused hierarchy, a product that is too small, a product that does not cross the edge, a stiff edge crossing, messy type, something that looks like a whole poster rather than a label card, scattered information, and piled-up selling points.
